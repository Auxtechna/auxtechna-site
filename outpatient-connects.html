Why Outpatient Connects?

Everything a trust needs to prescribe, check and route outpatient medicines electronically

Sign once, at prescribing

The prescriber signs at the point of prescribing with an advanced electronic signature; the content is SHA-256 locked, so it's tamper-evident from the moment it's created. No separate signing step.

National NHS EPS routing

Community-routed prescriptions transmit to the patient's nominated pharmacy over the national Electronic Prescription Service — built on the same CIS2 / PDS / FHIR foundations as HomeCare Connects.

Cost-effective supply routing

A pricing engine compares the real cost of each supply route — national Drug Tariff for the community, the trust's own contract prices for the hospital pharmacy — and recommends the cheapest, showing the pounds saved on every prescription.

Generic-first, wider formulary

Prescribe generically from a wide dm+d-backed formulary (substance → strength → form), with brand an optional pick. Each site chooses its formulary mode: its own curated list, the full dm+d catalogue, or a hybrid.

Pharmacist clinical check

A pharmacist reviews the prescription and passes-and-sends in one action, or sends it back to the prescriber with a query. The prescriber can amend and re-sign in place — a clean, closed loop with no paper.

Safety flags where they matter

Allergy checks, dose-range guards, an off-formulary-dose alert that forces a clinical check, and a clear "unlicensed medicine" badge at both prescribing and checking — the right warning in front of the right person.

Titration & dose escalation

Structured dose-escalation schedules for drugs that titrate — each step's dose, frequency and duration held against the drug, so an escalating regimen is prescribed faithfully rather than as free text.

Repeat medicines

A patient repeat-medicines list with a specialty-scoped worklist that shows what's due — overdue and soonest first — and re-issues a fresh, signed prescription in a click.

Prescribe by diagnosis

Curated order sets tied to a diagnosis drop a whole bundle of pre-configured drug lines into the prescription, editable before signing — faster, more consistent prescribing for common presentations.

NHS identity & demographics

Prescriber identity and MFA via NHS CIS2; patient demographics and registered GP verified from PDS. The people and the patient are who the record says they are.

Multi-specialty, per-consultant

Prescribing organisation ODS per specialty, per-specialty days-supply defaults, and specialty-scoped repeats and workflow filters — each clinic sees and manages its own patients.

Hospital dispensing workflow

Medicines routed to the hospital's own pharmacy land in a dispensing worklist, so non-EPS supply is tracked to completion alongside the community prescriptions — one view of the whole outpatient supply picture.

How a prescription flows

  1. Prescribe from the formulary (generic-first), with dose, quantity or days, and the cheapest supply route suggested automatically.
  2. Sign once — the prescription is AES-signed and locked at the click.
  3. Clinical check — a pharmacist reviews and passes it, or sends it back for amendment.
  4. Route & supply — community items transmit over NHS EPS to the nominated pharmacy; hospital items drop into the dispensing worklist.
